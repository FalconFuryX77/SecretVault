How it works

Three layers, from the outside in

Each layer covers a different moment: a quick look through your phone, being forced to unlock it, and a lab with your phone and time.

  1. Quick look

    A file manager that really is one

    • Browse, open, rename and delete your phone's files like any file manager.
    • No vault button, menu or wording anywhere in it. The way in is a triple-tap only you know about.
    • It never reacts to being picked up or shaken while locked, so handling it gives nothing away.
  2. Forced to unlock

    A decoy you can hand over

    • A second PIN opens a separate, ordinary-looking vault. Both PINs go into the same field.
    • Nothing in the decoy shows that another vault exists. The storage always has four key slots, used or not.
    • The real vault never overwrites the decoy, and the decoy never touches the real vault's space.
  3. Seized phone

    Encryption tied to your phone

    • Your PIN is never stored. It's stretched with Argon2id and mixed with a key locked inside your phone's secure hardware.
    • A copied vault can't be cracked on another computer, because every guess has to run on your phone.
    • No fingerprint unlock, on purpose. A finger can be pressed to a sensor by force. A PIN can't be taken that way, and only a PIN can choose the decoy.
When it counts

Built for the bad five minutes

Panic shake

Lock in one motion

Shake the phone hard, or just press the power button, and the open vault locks at once, clearing its keys from memory.

Auto-lock

Locks behind you

Leaving the app or putting the phone down locks the vault. Typing a long note keeps it open.

No screenshots

Blank in recent apps

Vault screens can't be captured and show blank in the app switcher. The cover app behaves normally.

Tamper-evident

Refuses altered files

Every 64 KB of every file is authenticated. A changed, swapped or cut-short file won't open.

Lockouts

Guessing gets slower

Five wrong tries lock it for 30 seconds, then a minute, then two, doubling up to an hour.

Quiet

Connects to one place

The app makes no connections until you set up backup, and then only to the server you entered. It's excluded from phone cloud backups.

Opens inside

No other app sees it

Photos, video, audio, PDFs, Word documents and text open right in the vault, and text files can be edited there. Nothing decrypted is saved to the phone.

Sealed-off reader

PDFs can't reach the vault

PDFs are drawn in a separate, locked-down process with no access to your files, the vault or the internet. A booby-trapped document gets nothing.

Private typing

Your keyboard forgets

What you type in the vault isn't learned by the keyboard, so it won't turn up later as a suggestion somewhere else.

Hidden recorder

Record without looking

With the screen off and the phone in your pocket, press volume-down three times to start recording, three more to stop. No unlocking, no screen.

Locked recording

Even your phone can't play it

Audio is encrypted as it's recorded, to a key only your vault holds. It moves into the vault the next time you unlock it; the decoy never sees it.

Crash-safe

Keeps what it caught

Recordings are saved every second. If the phone dies or is switched off mid-recording, everything up to the last second is kept.

Specs

Standard parts, documented

No home-made ciphers. Every piece is a well-studied building block, and the way they fit together is written down byte by byte so anyone can check it.

Read the container format

EncryptionAES-256-GCM, in 64 KB chunks bound to the file, position and length
Key stretchingArgon2id, 64 MiB, 3 passes
Device bindingHMAC-SHA256 under a non-exportable Android Keystore key (StrongBox where available)
UnlockPIN or passphrase only. No fingerprint, no face: those can be taken by force
StorageOne pre-allocated file of ciphertext and random filler. No headers, no markers; its size doesn't track what you store
DeletingOverwritten with random data and its key destroyed
SpeedUnlock in about 1.4 s; a 20 MB file imports in about 0.5 s (Galaxy M34)
Early · manual backup works, automatic is next

Getting your work out on a bad signal

The safest file is one that has already left the phone. SecretVault's encrypted sync sends files in small pieces that resume where they stopped, so even a few seconds of signal count.

The server only ever holds ciphertext. A news desk or NGO can run its own. And a phone taken from you can't delete what already got out.

Limits

What it can't do

If your safety depends on this, you should know where it stops.

  • It can't protect what you're made to unlock.If someone gets the real PIN out of you, they see the real vault. Give them the decoy instead.
  • The disguise fools a quick look, not an expert.Someone who knows this app can recognise it. The decoy is what holds up then.
  • A lab can tell there's encrypted data.It can't tell how much, or how many vaults.
  • It can't beat spyware.If your phone is already compromised, it can watch you unlock.
  • Use a real passphrase.A 6-digit PIN can be worked through on the phone itself in a few weeks. Longer is much safer.
  • Pictures aren't sealed off yet.Images open inside the app itself. So far only PDFs get the separate locked-down reader.
  • The recorder can be noticed.Android shows a green microphone dot while it records and lists the app as running while it's armed. If music is playing, the volume keys control the music instead.
  • Uninstalling erases the vault.The vault lives inside the app. Back it up to your server before removing or reinstalling.
  • Android only, and in beta.It hasn't had an independent security review yet.
